The theory, In a nutshell

So, this theory shows how two things can affect each other's outcomes without triggering a sequence of events to interact with each other or even directly interacting at all. DISCLAIMER: This theory works in ALL TIME, not REAL TIME!!

How does luck theory actually work?

Well, one of my earliest examples was "If you get struck by lightning, you have a lesser chance of winning the lottery" Of course, this isn't entirely true; the theory works in ALL TIME, not REAL TIME. And when I say that it works in all time, not real time, I mean that if you got struck by lightning (and somehow survived), and got a lottery ticket- the odds of you opening up the lottery ticket and winning have NOT changed, but in ALL TIME, the odds of that event happening HAVE lowered. This is because of the fact that the odds of you winning the lottery ALONE are easier to get in a lifetime than getting struck by lightning AND winning the lottery in a lifetime. Very confusing, isn't it?

What exactly do I mean by "All time vs. Real time"?

So, when I say Luck Theory works in all time, not real time, think of your life like a giant video game save file. "Real time" is you playing the game right now. If you buy a lottery ticket today, the game doesn't care if you got struck by lightning yesterday—the machine rolls the exact same numbers, meaning your real-time odds never change. But "ALL time" means zooming out to look at your entire playthrough from start to finish. If a data scientist looks at your whole life story, the odds of one single player getting hit by lightning AND winning the lottery before hitting "Game Over" are practically zero! Getting hit by lightning checks off one crazy rare box on your lifetime timeline, which makes your whole story mathematically insane. So, the lightning didn't magically rewrite the lottery machine today, but it completely changed the odds of your entire life movie! Pretty wild, right?
